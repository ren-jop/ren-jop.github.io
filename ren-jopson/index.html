AI in the build loop

AI is part of my process, not a decorative feature.

I use AI heavily across engineering and productivity: to shorten feedback loops, surface options faster and keep complex projects legible over time.

EngineeringFor Swift, Rust and embedded work, I use AI to reason through implementation options, inspect compiler errors, produce small patch plans, review integration points and generate hypotheses I can verify by building and testing.
Research and hardwareI use AI to synthesise datasheets and technical documentation, compare APIs or HAL approaches, sanity-check assumptions, and turn scattered source material into an actionable engineering plan.
ProductivityI use AI to transform rough notes into project handoffs, roadmaps, study material and structured knowledge; to connect planning with Focus sessions; and to reduce the overhead of maintaining systems such as Obsidian and Anki.
Control stays with the projectAI output is treated as a proposal. The code still has to compile, hardware assumptions still have to match documentation, and UI decisions still have to fit the actual workflow.

How I work

The goal is not to maximise output. It is to keep iteration fast without losing understanding of the system.

Build the smallest real thing

I prefer working code, real hardware or an actual interaction loop over speculative architecture.

Use AI to compress the search space

AI helps enumerate approaches, explain failures and surface edge cases so I can spend more time evaluating what survives contact with the project.

Keep evidence close

Compiler output, runtime behaviour, datasheets, measurements and user feedback outrank a plausible-looking generated answer.

The interesting part of AI is not replacing the work. It is changing how quickly you can move between an idea, a test and a better version.
